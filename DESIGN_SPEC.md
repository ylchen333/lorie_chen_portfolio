# Portfolio Rework Specification
### Positioning Lorie Chen as a Creative R&D / Creative Technologist

**Audience:** Codex / Future contributors
**Website:** https://loriechen.com
**Primary Goal:** Rewrite and reorganize the portfolio so that visitors perceive Lorie as a **Creative Technologist** working in **Creative R&D**—someone who explores emerging technologies by building prototypes that influence future creative tools, workflows, and products.

---

# Core Positioning

The portfolio should **NOT** read as:

- Fine artist who also codes
- Research engineer with artistic hobbies
- Creative coder showing cool projects
- Student portfolio

Instead it should communicate:

> Lorie investigates emerging AI, graphics, robotics, and interactive technologies by building experimental creative tools and experiences. Her work sits between research, engineering, design, and artistic practice, using prototypes to discover new workflows and interaction paradigms.

The portfolio should consistently answer the following question:

> **"How does this project help us understand the future of creative technology?"**

rather than

> "What did I make?"

---

# Core Narrative

Every page should reinforce the same R&D loop:

Emerging Technology
↓
Creative Question
↓
Prototype
↓
Experimentation
↓
Insights
↓
Implications for Future Creative Tools

Visitors should finish the website understanding that Lorie's value is not simply technical execution.

Her value is:

- identifying interesting questions
- rapidly prototyping
- synthesizing research
- understanding artists
- translating between engineering and creative practice
- discovering new product opportunities

# Standard Project Structure

Every project should follow approximately:

## Overview

Short summary.

---

## Question

What creative question motivated this work?

Examples:

How can AI become an editable creative material?

How should artists navigate 3D archives?

What happens when robotic systems become painting collaborators?

---

## Context

Why does this question matter?

Who cares?

What existing limitation motivated the project?

---

## Experiment

What prototype was built?

What was tested?

---

## Technical Approach

Engineering details.

Research.

Architecture.

Models.

Libraries.

Graphics.

Vision.

Interaction.

---

## Findings

What worked?

What failed?

What surprised you?

Creative R&D values failed experiments.

They should be included.

---

## Implications

This is the most important section.

Discuss:

How might these findings influence:

- future creative tools
- future products
- interaction paradigms
- artistic workflows
- research directions

Every project should conclude with:

"So what?"

# Show Process

Creative R&D portfolios benefit from process.

Show:

- sketches
- diagrams
- failed prototypes
- intermediate renders
- debugging
- screenshots
- notebooks
- experiments

The portfolio should feel like a lab notebook rather than only a gallery.

---

# Emphasize Failure

Failure is valuable.

Instead of hiding unsuccessful experiments:

Discuss:

- what was attempted
- why it failed
- what was learned

Interesting failures demonstrate genuine research.

---

# Work vs Playlab

Clarify the distinction.

## Work

Finished investigations.

Polished case studies.

High impact.

4–6 flagship projects.

---

## Playlab

Small explorations.

Quick prototypes.

Shader experiments.

Gaussian splat tests.

Audio-reactive sketches.

Segmentation tests.

ComfyUI workflows.

Tiny interaction experiments.

Graphics explorations.

Failed ideas.

Weekend prototypes.

# Product Thinking

This is currently underrepresented.

Each project should answer:

If this prototype became a product:

- who benefits?
- what workflow changes?
- what new capability exists?

Do NOT turn everything into startup marketing.

Instead discuss implications naturally.
