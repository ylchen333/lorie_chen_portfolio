Synth Splat is an audio-reactive 3D Gaussian Splatting project, built with a team. Sound drives the splat scene in real time, turning music into a live, navigable radiance field.

Built at the Worlds in Action Hackathon in Los Angeles, Synth Splat was a 48-hour project that explored the intersection of music and 3D graphics. The team used Spark.js for the 3D rendering, Meyda.js for audio feature extraction, and XGrids PortalCam for camera control. I was responsible for implementing the audio-reactive features, including mapping audio features to splat parameters and creating a dynamic camera system that responds to the music. In addition to the technical implementation, I also contributed to the overall design and user experience of the project, ensuring that the interface was intuitive for users and accessible through the web browser.

In collaboration with Steph Ng and Nick Pudjarminta. Steph Ng is a creative technologist with skills in both design and engineering, with a background in AR/VR. Currently building a startup in digital fashion. Nick Pudjarminta is a creative technologist who co-founded the award-winning mixed reality studio AmaVR, led cutting edge VR research at the USC World Building Media Lab, and facilitated Metaverse technical pipelines at Meta.

Workflow = Setlist preprocessing → Marble (generate/edit splats) → export ply/spz → web visualizer (Spark.js, shader/audio-reactive animation via Meyda.js)
